Qualcomm starts job cuts; some India staff get notices

The job cuts Qualcomm's CEO announced in October have begun, a report says. India and California are among the places affected.

Semicon Weekly · 11 November 2026
ShareWhatsAppTelegram
In short
  • Qualcomm has started the job cuts its CEO announced last month, according to people familiar with the plans.
  • Some employees in India have already got layoff notices. Reports said up to 1,000 India jobs could go, but sources say the real number is likely much lower.
  • Qualcomm did not comment. The exact number of cuts is not confirmed.

Key numbers

~57,000Qualcomm employees worldwide (Sept 2026)
Up to 1,000India jobs in reports (likely much lower)
-26%Phone chip revenue fall, latest quarter

What happened

  • Investing.com reported on 10 November that Qualcomm has begun planned job cuts across the world, citing people familiar with the company's plans.
  • Last month, CEO Cristiano Amon told staff in a memo that the company would make "performance-focused" job cuts. Sources said the cuts are not linked to Qualcomm's AI plans.
  • Some employees in India have already received layoff notices. Reports from India said up to 1,000 people could be affected, but sources said the real number is expected to be much lower. In California, Qualcomm is expected to file official layoff notices in the coming days.
  • Qualcomm has about 57,000 employees in 38 countries. Its phone chip (handset) revenue fell 26% in its latest quarter, while total revenue was $10.44 billion. The company declined to comment on the job cuts.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • Qualcomm has some of its biggest teams in Hyderabad and Bengaluru, so these cuts matter for India's chip job market.
  • It shows how a slowdown in one business, here phone chips, can lead to job cuts even at a very large and profitable company.

Why it is good for freshers

  • Even big chip firms cut jobs when one business slows, so freshers should build strong basics and apply to many companies, not just one.
  • Skills like RTL design, verification and physical design are used across many chip companies, so they help you move if one company cuts jobs.
  • Keep track of company news before interviews and offers, and keep your options open.

Why it is good for India

  • India is home to one of Qualcomm's largest engineering teams, and the reported number of India cuts is likely lower than early reports.
  • Other chip companies and new Indian chip projects are still hiring, so trained engineers have more choices than before.

  • Performance-focused cuts: Job cuts where the company says it is choosing people based on their yearly work reviews.
