Bengaluru firm makes Indian part for military aircraft

A part India used to import in small lots is now built in Bengaluru, and it passed a strict military test standard.

Semicon Weekly · 4 November 2026
In short
  • izmo Microsystems, the chip unit of izmo Ltd, has built and qualified a 'pitch detector' at its Bengaluru facility.
  • This part senses how much an aircraft's nose tilts up or down and sends the data to its compass system.
  • The company rebuilt it without the original drawings, and it passed a strict military reliability standard.

What happened

  • izmo Microsystems built and qualified a pitch detector at its Bengaluru facility.
  • The part senses the up-or-down tilt of an aircraft's nose and sends this data to the compass system.
  • India used to buy this part from abroad in small, irregular lots.
  • The company rebuilt it without the original drawings, and it passed a strict military reliability standard.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • Defence aircraft need parts that keep working in heat, cold and shaking.
  • Making the part at home means India does not depend on irregular imports.

Why it is good for freshers

  • Replacing imported defence electronics needs engineers who understand circuits, packaging and testing, which is a good path for ECE freshers.
  • Learn reliability testing, PCB and packaging basics.
  • Reverse engineering and documentation skills are valued in defence electronics.

Why it is good for India

  • More defence electronics made in India supports self-reliance.
  • Small and mid-size Indian firms gain skills in high-reliability parts.

Words in this story

  • Pitch: how much an aircraft's nose points up or down.
  • Avionics: the electronics inside an aircraft.
  • Qualified: tested and approved to meet a required standard.
