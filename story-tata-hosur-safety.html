Three workers die at Tata Electronics' Hosur plant; probe ordered

The workers were clearing sludge from a wastewater tank at a utility unit inside the iPhone-parts factory. One went in and collapsed, and the two who tried to help were also overcome.

Semicon Weekly · 11 November 2026
ShareWhatsAppTelegram
In short
  • Three contract workers died on the evening of 9 November at Tata Electronics' plant near Hosur, Tamil Nadu, which makes iPhone parts.
  • They were cleaning a wastewater treatment tank and were overcome by toxic gas and low oxygen.
  • The Krishnagiri district collector has launched a full inquiry into the breach of safety rules; police are investigating.

What happened

  • On Monday evening, 9 November 2026, workers were clearing sludge from a wastewater treatment tank at a utility unit run by Nalco Water (an Ecolab company) inside Tata Electronics' Hosur plant.
  • Police said one worker opened the tank to inspect it and collapsed from the fumes. Two colleagues who tried to help were also overcome; a fourth crew member raised the alarm.
  • The three men were pulled out and taken to a hospital in Hosur, where they were declared dead. They were contract workers engaged through a sub-contractor.
  • Krishnagiri collector C Dinesh Kumar said a full inquiry has been launched into the breach of safety rules. Tamil Nadu's industries minister promised a thorough investigation, and Nalco Water said it is working with the authorities. Tata Electronics had not commented when reports were published.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • Closed tanks can hold toxic gases and very little oxygen, even when they look safe from outside.
  • In such accidents, people who rush in to help often become victims too. Rescue must only be done with breathing equipment.

Why it is good for freshers

  • Factory and fab jobs are growing in India, so freshers should learn safety rules first and never enter a closed tank without gas checks and proper gear.
  • In any plant job, ask about the permit-to-work system, gas testing and the emergency plan on your first day — it is normal and expected.
  • Fabs use many chemicals and gases; safety training is part of the job, not extra work.

Why it is good for India

  • India's electronics and chip factories are growing fast, and safety rules must grow with them.
  • Contract workers often do the riskiest jobs; strong rules and training must cover them too.

Words in this story

  • Asphyxiation: Death or injury caused by not getting enough oxygen, for example from toxic gas or low oxygen in a closed space.
  • Confined space: A closed or partly closed area like a tank or pit, where gas can collect and it is hard to get out.
  • Permit-to-work: A written safety check that must be done and signed before risky work, like entering a tank, can begin.
