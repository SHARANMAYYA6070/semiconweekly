India may invite Taiwan and Singapore institutes to train chip engineers

World-class chip research institutes could set up training centres in India under Chip Mission 2.0.

Semicon Weekly · 5 November 2026
ShareWhatsAppTelegram
In short
  • The government may invite Taiwan's ITRI and Singapore's A*STAR to set up chip training centres in India.
  • Germany's Fraunhofer is also on the list.
  • This is part of Chip Mission 2.0, which aims to train 1 lakh engineers in six years.

Key numbers

3Institutes named: ITRI, A*STAR, Fraunhofer
1 lakhEngineers to train under Mission 2.0
6 yearsTraining target period

What happened

  • The government is considering inviting foreign chip research institutes to train engineers in India.
  • The institutes named are Taiwan's ITRI and Singapore's A*STAR.
  • Germany's Fraunhofer is also on the list.
  • The plan is part of India Semiconductor Mission 2.0, which aims to train 1 lakh engineers in six years. It is a proposal for now, not a final decision.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • These institutes have helped build chip industries in their own countries.
  • Hands-on fab training is hard to get in colleges, so expert centres fill a big gap.

Why it is good for freshers

  • World-class chip training could come to India, so students may learn fab skills without going abroad.
  • Watch for training programmes and fellowships if this plan goes ahead.
  • Process, equipment and packaging skills are what fabs need most.

Why it is good for India

  • More trained engineers make India more attractive for new chip plants.
  • Partnering with proven institutes can speed up India's chip learning curve.

Words in this story

  • ITRI: Industrial Technology Research Institute, Taiwan's big public research body that helped start its chip industry.
  • A*STAR: Singapore's Agency for Science, Technology and Research.
  • Fraunhofer: a large German network of applied research institutes.
