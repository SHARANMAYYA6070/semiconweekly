How to become a DFT engineer: a step-by-step roadmap for freshers

What to learn, in what order, which tools and projects, and what interviewers ask: a clear 14-week path from ECE student to DFT engineer.

📝 Applying? Make your resume interview-ready first: VLSI fresher resume guide with samples →

Semicon Weekly · Updated 5 November 2026 · For ECE / EEE students and VLSI freshers
What you doAdd test logic (scan, BIST, JTAG) so every manufactured chip can be tested quickly for defects.
Main skillsFault models, scan, ATPG, MBIST, JTAG, timing basics, scripting.
Fresher payAbout ₹5.5–9 LPA in the middle of the market; more at top product companies.

The roadmap

  1. 1

    Get the digital basics strongWeeks 1–3

    • Flip-flops, FSMs, combinational logic and timing basics. See digital electronics questions.
    • Verilog: read and write simple RTL and testbenches.
    • Why chips need testing: manufacturing defects, yield and test cost.
  2. 2

    Learn fault models and ATPGWeeks 3–6

    • Fault models: stuck-at, transition (at-speed), bridging and path delay faults.
    • Controllability and observability: why some logic is hard to test.
    • ATPG: how patterns are generated, fault coverage vs test coverage, and pattern count.
  3. 3

    Learn scan designWeeks 6–9

    • Scan flip-flops (mux-D), scan chains, scan enable, shift and capture modes.
    • Scan insertion rules: clocks, resets, latches and non-scan flops.
    • Scan compression and why it cuts test time and tester memory.
    • At-speed test with launch-on-shift and launch-on-capture.
  4. 4

    Learn memory and boundary testWeeks 9–12

    • Memory BIST (MBIST): march algorithms and repair basics.
    • Logic BIST (LBIST) at a high level.
    • JTAG (IEEE 1149.1) boundary scan: TAP controller, instructions and boundary cells.
  5. 5

    Learn DFT timing and simulationWeeks 12–14

    • Timing for scan mode: shift clock frequency and hold issues on scan paths. See STA questions.
    • Simulating ATPG patterns on the netlist and debugging mismatches.
    • How DFT fits into the flow: after synthesis, before and during physical design.
  6. 6

    Build your portfolio and applyWeeks 14+

    • GitHub: a small design with hand-inserted scan, a simple TAP controller, or a march-C MBIST in Verilog, with testbenches.
    • Be ready to explain stuck-at vs transition faults and how scan works with a drawing.
    • Practise answers out loud with the AI Interview Bot.
    • Apply to fresher jobs and ask for a referral.

Tools

Free tools to practise at home

Icarus Verilog / Verilator and EDA Playground for scan, TAP and MBIST RTL; Yosys for synthesis; open fault-simulation tools and textbook exercises for ATPG concepts.

Industry tools (you will meet them at work)

Scan insertion and ATPG: Synopsys TestMAX (DFT Compiler / TetraMAX), Siemens Tessent, Cadence Modus. MBIST: Tessent MemoryBIST. Simulation: VCS, Xcelium, Questa.

Projects to put on your resume

Pick two or three and finish them well. A finished project you can explain beats five half-done ones. Put the code on GitHub with a short README and waveforms.

  1. Scan chain on a small counterBeginner
    Replace flops with mux-D scan flops, shift a pattern in and out, and show it in waveforms.
  2. Fault simulation by handBeginner
    Take a small circuit, list stuck-at faults, find test patterns and compute fault coverage.
  3. JTAG TAP controller in VerilogIntermediate
    Implement the 16-state TAP FSM with IDCODE and BYPASS and a testbench.
  4. March-C MBIST for a small RAMIntermediate
    Write the march algorithm in RTL, inject a fault and show it is detected.
  5. Scan compression modelAdvanced
    Model a simple decompressor/compactor and compare test time with plain scan.

Interview topics to prepare

  • Digital basics: digital electronics questions
  • Verilog and RTL: Verilog questions and RTL design questions
  • STA basics: scan-mode timing and hold — STA questions
  • Expect: "Explain stuck-at and transition faults", "How does a scan chain work?", "Why does scan compression help?", "Draw the JTAG TAP states".

Tips that make a difference

  • DFT is a smaller field than DV, so fewer people prepare for it. A focused DFT profile stands out.
  • Draw everything: a scan flop, a scan chain, the TAP state machine. Interviewers love whiteboard answers.
  • Learn enough STA to talk about scan shift timing and hold fixes.
  • Many DFT engineers start in DV or PD teams; the digital and timing basics carry over.
