Vaishnaw: no place for non-serious chip scheme applicants

The government wants companies with real technology and long-term plans, not names on a list. Only 3 of Semicon 2.0's 6 parts are open for applications so far.

Semicon Weekly · 8 November 2026
ShareWhatsAppTelegram
In short
  • IT minister Ashwini Vaishnaw says the chip, electronics parts and phone schemes are only for serious, long-term companies.
  • He asked officials to remove 18–19 non-serious applications from the electronics parts scheme (ECMS).
  • The new Semicon 2.0 portal is open for 3 of its 6 parts; design, R&D and talent have not opened yet.

Key numbers

18–19weak ECMS applications to be removed
3 of 6Semicon 2.0 parts open for applications
12new ECMS projects cleared, worth over ₹7,500 crore

What happened

  • Ashwini Vaishnaw spoke in New Delhi on 6 November 2026, the day the Semicon 2.0 application portal opened.
  • He said the portal only accepts applications that are complete in every way, so that only serious companies take part.
  • He asked the IT secretary to remove 18–19 non-serious players from the electronics components manufacturing scheme (ECMS) list. An official said some applications lacked the right approvals or technology partners.
  • The portal is open for machines and materials, fabs, and packaging. The other three parts — design, R&D and talent — have no guidelines yet.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • The money will go to companies that can really build plants and products, which makes the jobs they create more likely to be real and lasting.
  • The same strict rule will apply to the electronics parts scheme and the new mobile phone scheme.

Why it is good for freshers

  • The design, R&D and talent parts of Semicon 2.0 have not opened yet; they are the parts closest to student and fresher careers, so watch for them.
  • Companies that pass these checks are serious about building in India, so their hiring plans are worth tracking.
  • Fab, packaging and machine-and-materials units are open first, so manufacturing and test skills are useful right now.

Why it is good for India

  • India wants plants that really get built, not projects that stay on paper.
  • A stricter filter can help the chip mission spend its large budget on companies that deliver.

Words in this story

  • ECMS: Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme: government support for factories that make electronic parts in India.
  • Semicon 2.0: The second phase of the India Semiconductor Mission, with support for fabs, packaging, machines, materials, design, R&D and talent.
  • Application portal: A website where companies send their full plans and papers to ask for government support.
