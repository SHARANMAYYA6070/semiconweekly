Chip design

India aims to build its own GPU chip in about two years

The plan is part of India's push to depend less on foreign AI chips. The government also wants to sell the Indian-made SHAKTI and VEGA processors, which use the open RISC-V design.

Semicon Weekly · 8 November 2026

  • He called it a development target, not a fixed launch date.
  • India also plans to sell its home-grown SHAKTI and VEGA RISC-V processors.

What happened

  • MeitY Secretary S Krishnan spoke about India's AI and chip plans in an interview with ETGovernment, published on 4 November 2026.
  • He said India aims to develop its own GPU chip in about 24 months under India Semiconductor Mission 2.0, and called it a development target, not a firm launch date.
  • He said only a few companies in the world make AI chips, which keeps prices high, so India wants its own.
  • India also wants to sell its home-grown SHAKTI and VEGA processors, which are built on the open RISC-V design, and to use trusted Indian chips in things like CCTV cameras, drones and smart meters.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • A GPU is the main chip used to train and run AI, so an Indian GPU would cut dependence on foreign suppliers.
  • Chip Mission 2.0, approved on 15 July 2026 with ₹1,27,500 crore, covers chip design as well as factories and packaging.

Why it is good for freshers

  • A home-grown GPU and RISC-V chips will need Indian engineers in RTL design, verification and physical design, so these skills will stay in demand.
  • Learning RISC-V is a good start: it is open, free to study, and used in India's SHAKTI and VEGA processors.
  • Under the first design scheme, 24 companies got support and 15 of them later raised funding — design startups are growing.

Why it is good for India

  • Owning the full AI chain — from chips to servers to apps — makes India less dependent on other countries.
  • Selling Indian processors can bring more chip design work, and jobs, to Indian companies.

Words in this story

  • GPU: Graphics Processing Unit: a chip with thousands of small cores, used for graphics and for training and running AI.
  • RISC-V: An open, free instruction set (the basic 'language' of a processor) that anyone can use to design a CPU.
  • SHAKTI and VEGA: India's own RISC-V processors, made by IIT Madras (SHAKTI) and C-DAC (VEGA).
