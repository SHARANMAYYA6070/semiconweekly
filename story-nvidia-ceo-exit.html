Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; Colette Kress takes charge

Nvidia's co-founder stepped down with immediate effect. Its finance chief is now running the company for the time being, and results are still due on 17 November.

Semicon Weekly · 14 November 2026
ShareWhatsAppTelegram
In short
  • Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's President, CEO and board member on 12 November, with immediate effect.
  • CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO and will stay CFO too.
  • The board will search for a permanent CEO, and results come on 17 November as planned.

Key numbers

12 NovHuang resigned
Nov 2026Kress named interim CEO
17 NovQ3 results date

What happened

  • Nvidia said Jensen Huang resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer and board member, effective immediately.
  • Colette Kress, Executive Vice President and CFO, agreed to be interim CEO until a permanent successor is named. She joined Nvidia in 2013.
  • The board will look at candidates from inside and outside the company. Harvey C Jones, a former Synopsys CEO, was named the board's first-ever Chair.
  • Nvidia said its operations, customer commitments and product plans will continue without interruption.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • Nvidia makes the GPUs that power most AI data centres, so a change at the top is watched by the whole chip industry.
  • The 17 November results will show how its business is doing after the leadership change.

Why it is good for freshers

  • Nvidia is one of the biggest chip design employers in India; nothing has changed in hiring yet, but freshers should follow the 17 November results and keep applying to many companies.
  • Leadership changes do not change the basic skills chip companies need: RTL, verification, physical design and software.
  • Applying to many companies keeps your options open when news at one big company is uncertain.

Why it is good for India

  • Big Indian design teams work on Nvidia's products, so stable operations matter for engineers here.
  • The company says its product roadmap continues, so ongoing work is not expected to stop.
