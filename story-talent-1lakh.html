India plans to train 1 lakh chip engineers in six years

After meeting its first training goal, India wants many more chip engineers, technicians and operators.

Semicon Weekly · 3 November 2026
In short
  • India met its goal of training 85,000 chip engineers under the first Chip Mission.
  • Under Mission 2.0, the plan is to train 1 lakh more engineers over the next six years.
  • Experts say companies also need technicians, operators and diploma holders with hands-on training.

Key numbers

85,000Engineers trained under the first mission
1 lakhMore planned under Mission 2.0
6 yearsTime for the new goal

What happened

  • India set a goal of training 85,000 chip engineers under the first India Semiconductor Mission, and met it.
  • Under Mission 2.0, the plan is to train 1 lakh more engineers over six years.
  • Experts say the industry also needs skilled technicians, machine operators and diploma holders.
  • They say hands-on, practical training is what companies want most.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • New chip plants and design centres cannot run without trained people.
  • Training technicians and operators, not only engineers, fills real gaps in factories.

Why it is good for freshers

  • The government and industry want lakhs of trained chip engineers, so practical skills and lab work will make freshers stand out.
  • Do real projects: RTL designs, testbenches, FPGA builds, and put them on GitHub.
  • Look for free and government training programmes linked to the chip mission.

Why it is good for India

  • A big trained workforce is one of India's strongest selling points for chip companies.
  • Skills spread beyond big cities through colleges and diploma institutes.

Words in this story

  • Mission 2.0: the second phase of the India Semiconductor Mission.
  • Hands-on training: learning by doing real work on tools and machines, not only theory.
