India clears ₹1.25 lakh crore for Chip Mission 2.0

The second phase of India's chip mission is bigger, lasts longer, and gives more support to chip design.

Semicon Weekly · Reported 15 July 2026
ShareWhatsAppTelegram
In short
  • India's government approved ₹1.25 lakh crore for the next phase of the India Semiconductor Mission (ISM 2.0).
  • The first phase had ₹76,000 crore. The new one is about 1.6 times bigger.
  • This time the money also goes to machines, materials, chemicals, gases and Indian chip designs, not only factories.

Key numbers

₹1.25 lakh crMoney for ISM 2.0
₹76,000 crMoney for ISM 1.0 (started Dec 2021)
12Chip projects approved in phase 1
₹1.64 lakh crInvestment planned in those projects
12 yearsNew programme period (earlier 5 years)

What happened

  • In early July 2026, the Finance Ministry cleared the ₹1.25 lakh crore budget for ISM 2.0.
  • On 15 July 2026, reports said the Union Cabinet also approved it.
  • The programme period was made longer, from 5 years to 12 years, so big investors can plan for the long term.
  • Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said the mission now covers the whole electronics chain, and that chip design companies will get special focus.

What ISM 2.0 pays for

  • Factories: chip fabs, and plants that pack and test chips.
  • Machines and materials: making chip-making equipment, raw materials, chemicals and special gases in India.
  • Chip design: Indian-owned chip designs (called IP) and design companies.
  • Talent: building skills and turning designs into real products.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • Long, steady support makes it easier for companies to invest crores of rupees in India.
  • Covering machines, materials and design means India depends less on other countries for each step.

Why it is good for freshers

  • More support for design companies can mean more RTL design, verification and physical design jobs.
  • New factories and supplier plants need process, equipment, testing and quality engineers.
  • Skills to build now: Verilog/SystemVerilog, digital design basics, scripting (Python, TCL).

Why it is good for India

  • Chips go into phones, cars, defence and payments. Making them here makes India safer from supply problems.
  • Indian-owned chip designs mean India keeps more of the value, not only the assembly work.
