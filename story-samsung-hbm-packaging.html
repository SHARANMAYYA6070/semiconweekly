Samsung starts $4.5 billion AI memory packaging plant in Korea

The new plant in Asan will package HBM, the stacked memory placed next to AI chips, and is planned to start a year early.

Semicon Weekly · 13 November 2026
ShareWhatsAppTelegram
In short
  • Samsung held a groundbreaking ceremony on 12 November for an HBM packaging plant in Asan, South Korea, worth 6 trillion won (about $4.5 billion).
  • South Korea's President Lee Jae Myung said about 40 trillion won (about $30 billion) of advanced machines will follow.
  • Samsung plans to start the line in June 2028, a year earlier than first planned.

Key numbers

₩6 trillionPlant investment, about $4.5 billion
₩40 trillionPlanned equipment investment, about $30 billion
June 2028Planned start of the HBM line

What happened

  • Samsung Electronics broke ground on 12 November on a new HBM chip packaging plant in Asan, South Korea.
  • President Lee Jae Myung spoke at the ceremony and said the 6 trillion won plant will be followed by about 40 trillion won of advanced equipment, added in stages.
  • Samsung said the line is planned to start in June 2028, a year ahead of its earlier plan, because demand for server memory is rising.
  • The plant will have three floors of clean rooms, about 31,000 square metres in total, as per the report.

Why it matters

Why this is useful

  • It shows how much money is going into packaging, the final step where chips are assembled and connected.
  • AI chips need HBM memory, so more HBM capacity helps AI hardware supply.

Why it is good for freshers

  • Advanced packaging is the same kind of work India's new OSAT plants are starting, so packaging and test skills are in demand worldwide.
  • Learning how memory chips are stacked and connected (like through-silicon vias) is a useful skill for packaging roles.
  • Global memory makers are growing fast, which can mean more process, equipment and test jobs.

Why it is good for India

  • India's new packaging plants can learn from how leaders like Samsung build advanced packaging lines.
  • As memory demand grows, India's packaging and test plants may find more work in this supply chain.

Words in this story

  • HBM (high-bandwidth memory): Memory chips stacked on top of each other and placed next to an AI chip to move data very fast
  • Packaging: The step where a finished chip is put in a protective case and connected so it can be used
  • Clean room: A very clean, dust-free room where chips are made or packaged
